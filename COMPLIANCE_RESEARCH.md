# Exam-Policy & Compliance Research

**Subject:** TI-84 Plus CE Python Edition study/practice-exam programs (this repository)
**Purpose:** Establish, from primary sources, exactly what may and may not be claimed about where
these programs are permitted, so that marketing is accurate and defensible.
**All sources accessed:** 2026-08-12 (unless a different date is noted on the source itself).

> **How to read this document.** Every material statement is tagged:
>
> - **[VERIFIED]** — quoted directly from the exam authority's own published policy, at the URL given.
> - **[INFERRED]** — a conclusion I drew by combining verified sources. The reasoning is shown so you
>   can judge it yourself. Do **not** put an inference in marketing copy as if it were a quote.
> - **[UNVERIFIED]** — could not be confirmed from a primary source. Treat as unknown.
>
> This document is research, not legal advice. Policies change at least annually. Re-check every
> cited URL before any marketing push, and again before each exam season.

---

## 0. Executive summary

| Authority | Is the TI-84 Plus CE Python allowed as a device? | Are stored programs / formulas allowed? | Marketing verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AP Exams (College Board)** | **Yes — named on the approved list** | **Memory need not be cleared; no published prohibition on stored programs** | Strongest honest claim available. Still cannot say "AP-approved programs." |
| **SAT / PSAT (College Board SAT Suite)** | Yes (device not prohibited) | **No — algebra-functionality programs must be removed; saved formulas must be cleared** | Do **not** market for in-test use. |
| **ACT / ACT WorkKeys** | Yes (device not prohibited) | **Mostly no** — allowed programs must be single-purpose and ≤ 25 logical lines; all *documents* removed | Do **not** market for in-test use. |
| **NCEES FE / PE / FS / PS** | **No — TI-84 is flatly prohibited** | N/A | **Never** market to engineering-licensure candidates. |
| **IB Diploma Programme** | Yes (non-CAS TI-84 family is permitted) | **No — programs must be removed or blocked; memories cleared** | Study-only. |
| **CLEP** | N/A — no personal calculator admitted at all | N/A | Study-only (though CLEP itself uses an on-screen TI-84 Plus CE). |
| **GED** | No personal graphing calculator | N/A | Do not market. |
| **University / course exams** | Instructor's discretion | Instructor's discretion | Study-only; tell buyers to ask. |

**The three headline facts:**

1. **AP is genuinely permissive, and much more so than the current README disclaimer assumes.**
   College Board states in writing that memories do **not** need to be cleared, and the TI-84 Plus
   CE Python is **named** on the approved list.
2. **The FE/PE verdict is absolute and unambiguous.** NCEES permits exactly six calculator families,
   none of which is a TI-84. Any FE/PE marketing would be a false statement of fact.
3. **Press-to-Test very likely *deletes* Python programs rather than hiding them.** This is a
   product-support fact the owner must disclose, not just a policy fact.

### 1.2 The memory question — the single most important finding

**[VERIFIED]** AP Students page, under "Important to know":

> "You don't need to clear your calculators' memories before or after the exam."

**[VERIFIED]** AP Central page (the version proctors and coordinators read), in the general bullets:

> "Calculator memories do not need to be cleared before or after the exam."

**[VERIFIED]** AP Central page, under "For security reasons, some calculators require special
instructions":

> "Calculators with built-in physical constants, metric conversions, and physics, chemistry, or
> mathematics formulas are permitted. Calculator memories do not need to be cleared before or after
> the exam."

**[VERIFIED]** The only memory-related prohibition College Board states is about *removing exam
content*, not about what is stored beforehand. AP Central:

> "Students must not use calculator memories to take test materials out of the room. Students who
> attempt to remove test materials from the room by any method will have their exam scores canceled."

And AP Students, under "Exam Day Rules":

> "You can't use calculator memories to take test materials out of the room. If you try to take test
> materials out of the room by any method, your scores will be canceled."

### 1.3 What College Board does *not* say — read this before writing copy

**This is the part the competitor gets wrong.** mcstutoring.com's page
(<https://mcstutoring.com/pages/ap-calculator-policy>, accessed 2026-08-12) asserts that College
Board "explicitly permits calculators with memory" and that programs are "explicitly allowed."

**[VERIFIED]** College Board's calculator policy contains **no affirmative sentence permitting
user-written programs.** I read both the student-facing and coordinator-facing pages in full. There
is no sentence of the form "programs are allowed."

**[INFERRED]** The permissive conclusion for AP rests on three verified facts, not on an explicit
permission:

1. Memories explicitly need not be cleared (quoted above, twice, in two different documents).
2. The "Unapproved Calculators and Technology" list is entirely **device-capability** based — QWERTY
   keypads, wireless/Bluetooth/cellular, internet access, cameras, audio/video recording, stylus
   input, touch screens not on the approved list, paper tape, power cords, hardware peripherals.
   Stored user programs appear nowhere on it.
3. Calculators with built-in formulas are affirmatively permitted (quoted above), which shows
   College Board does not treat "formulas available on the device" as an unfair aid on AP Exams.

That is a strong inference and I believe it is correct. But the honest framing in marketing is
*"College Board does not require you to clear your calculator's memory"* (a quote), **not**
*"College Board explicitly allows programs"* (not a quote, and not something College Board says).

**[VERIFIED — and note this carefully]** College Board *does* regulate software in one place. The
unapproved list includes:

> "Models with QWERTY (i.e., typewriter-like) keypads as part of hardware **or software** (e.g.,
> TI-92 Plus, Voyage 200)"

So College Board contemplates that software can make an otherwise-approved calculator unapproved.
None of the programs in this repository present a QWERTY keyboard, so this is not a problem here —
but it disproves any claim that College Board "doesn't care what software is on the device."

**[VERIFIED]** The AP Exam Terms and Conditions separately prohibit students from attempting to
"access any prohibited aids, including prohibited formula sheets and prohibited scratch paper" and
from any attempt to "cheat or otherwise obtain an unfair advantage on the Test." What counts as a
*prohibited* aid on the calculator is defined by the calculator policy, which is permissive about
memory. **[INFERRED]** These two documents are consistent: the general anti-cheating clause does not
override the specific, twice-stated rule that memories need not be cleared.

### 1.4 Device approval — the TI-84 Plus CE Python specifically

**[VERIFIED]** The TI-84 Plus CE Python is named on College Board's approved handheld graphing
calculator list, on **both** the AP Students page and the AP Central "2026-27 List of Approved
Handheld Graphing Calculators," as:

> "TI-84 Plus CE Python\*"

The `TI-84 Plus CE-T Python Edition*` is also listed. The asterisk means, per College Board's own
footnote:

> "Graphing calculators with the expected built-in capabilities for AP Calculus are indicated with
> an asterisk."

So the device is not merely tolerated — it is approved *and* flagged as meeting the AP Calculus
capability expectations. It is likewise on the AP Precalculus subset list.

**[VERIFIED]** Wireless/QWERTY/CAS concerns do not apply to this device: it has no wireless,
Bluetooth, or cellular capability, no camera, and an ALPHA-key (not QWERTY) layout — and it is on
the approved list, which settles the question regardless.

### 1.5 Per-subject calculator rules (2025-26 / 2026-27)

**[VERIFIED]** From the AP Central table:

| Course | Calculator type allowed | MCQ | FRQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calculus AB | Graphing calculator; built-in Desmos graphing via Bluebook | Required for Part B | Required for Part A |
| Calculus BC | Graphing calculator; built-in Desmos graphing via Bluebook | Required for Part B | Required for Part A |
| Precalculus | Graphing calculator; built-in Desmos graphing via Bluebook | Required for Part B | Required for Part A |
| Statistics | Graphing calculator with statistical capabilities; built-in Desmos graphing via Bluebook | Expected | Expected |
| Chemistry | Scientific or graphing calculator; built-in Desmos scientific or graphing; 4-function allowed but not recommended | Allowed | Allowed |
| Physics 1 | 4-function, scientific, or graphing | Allowed | Allowed |
| Physics 2 | 4-function, scientific, or graphing | Allowed | Allowed |
| Physics C: Mechanics | 4-function, scientific, or graphing | Allowed | Allowed |
| Physics C: E&M | 4-function, scientific, or graphing | Allowed | Allowed |

**[VERIFIED]** Important structural caveat, from AP Central:

> "Note that for Calculus AB, Calculus BC, and Precalculus, Desmos calculators will only be
> available in the calculator-required parts of the exam. These exams continue to have parts where
> no calculator is allowed."

So on AP Calculus and AP Precalculus, roughly half the exam is calculator-free — a program library
is worth nothing on those sections. Any marketing that implies calculator tools help across a whole
AP Calculus exam is misleading.

**[VERIFIED]** Students may bring up to two permitted handheld calculators in addition to the
Bluebook Desmos calculator; calculators may not be shared; communication between calculators is
prohibited; proctors are required to check handheld calculators before the exam.

**[VERIFIED]** Announced changes worth tracking (AP Central, "Update for 2027"):

> "Update for 2027 AP Biology and AP Environmental Science Exams: Handheld calculators with storage
> capabilities such as graphing calculators are not allowed."

> "Update for 2027 AP Statistics Exams: Nongraphing calculators are not allowed, even those with the
> required statistics computational capabilities."

The Biology/Environmental Science change is the direction of travel to watch: College Board is
starting to exclude *storage-capable* calculators from specific exams. AP Macroeconomics and
Microeconomics already carry the note that "Handheld calculators with storage capabilities such as
scientific calculators and graphing calculators are not allowed."

**[VERIFIED]** For 2025-26 College Board states: "the policies regarding the types of calculators
allowed or required for each subject and how calculators can and can't be used are not changing for
the 2025-26 school year."

### 1.6 What I could not verify for AP

**[UNVERIFIED]** The *AP Coordinator's Manual* and the proctor scripts are the operative
instructions actually read aloud in the room. I could not obtain the 2025-26 or 2026-27 manual
directly. The published calculator policy is authoritative and is what coordinators are pointed to,
but I cannot rule out additional room-level instructions. This is a real (if small) residual risk
and is the reason marketing should still tell students to be ready for a proctor to ask.

## 2. SAT, PSAT/NMSQT, PSAT 10, PSAT 8/9 — College Board SAT Suite

### 2.1 Primary sources

- *SAT Calculator Policy* — <https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/what-to-bring-do/calculator-policy> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *PSAT/NMSQT Calculator Policy* — <https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/psat-nmsqt/taking-the-test/what-to-bring-leave/calculator-policy> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *SAT Testing Rules* — <https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/testing-rules> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *PSAT/NMSQT Testing Rules* — <https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/psat-nmsqt/testing-rules> (accessed 2026-08-12)

### 2.2 The decisive language

**[VERIFIED]** SAT Calculator Policy, "Rules for Handheld Calculators":

> "You must remove programs that have algebra functionality (e.g., solving equations, expanding
> algebraic expressions, factoring algebraic expressions, etc.) from your calculator."

> "You must remove any stored documents from your calculator."

**[VERIFIED]** SAT Calculator Policy, "Prohibited Calculators":

> "Calculators with apps, menus, or programs that perform symbolic algebra manipulation (e.g.,
> solving algebra equations symbolically)."

**[VERIFIED]** *SAT Testing Rules* — "Allowed Items," bullet on calculators:

> "Before testing, you will be asked to clear all saved formulas on a calculator you bring."

**[VERIFIED]** The identical sentence appears in the *PSAT/NMSQT Testing Rules*.

### 2.3 What this means for this product

**[INFERRED]** Several programs in this repository fall squarely inside the prohibited description:

- `quadratic_solver.py`, `linear_system_solver.py`, `newton_raphson.py`, `limit_evaluator.py`,
  `matrix_toolkit.py`, `quadratic_vertex_analyzer.py` — these solve equations. The SAT rule bars
  "programs that have algebra functionality (e.g., **solving equations** …)". Note the rule's
  parenthetical says *solving equations*, not merely *symbolic* manipulation, even though the
  separate prohibited-calculator bullet is narrower and speaks of symbolic manipulation. The
  stricter of the two is the operative instruction to the student.
- `formula_flashcards.py` — a stored formula reference. The Testing Rules instruction to "clear all
  saved formulas" is directly on point.

**[INFERRED]** Therefore: on the SAT, PSAT/NMSQT, PSAT 10, and PSAT 8/9, a student who leaves these
programs on a handheld calculator is not following the published rules. This must never be marketed
as SAT- or PSAT-usable.

**[UNVERIFIED / genuinely ambiguous]** Whether a TI-84 Python AppVar counts as a "stored document."
"Document" is TI-Nspire terminology (a `.tns` file); College Board does not define it. This
ambiguity does *not* rescue the situation, because the "programs that have algebra functionality"
rule and the "clear all saved formulas" instruction are unambiguous on their own.

### 2.4 Other SAT Suite facts worth knowing

**[VERIFIED]** There is no longer an approved-model list, only a prohibited list. From the SAT
policy FAQ:

> "We no longer maintain a list of graphing calculators that are specifically permitted for use on
> the SAT Suite. Refer to the list of prohibited calculators that students may not use on the SAT
> Suite."

**[INFERRED]** Consequently, **you cannot truthfully say the TI-84 Plus CE Python is "on the
approved SAT list."** There is no such list. The accurate statement is that the device is not on the
prohibited list.

**[VERIFIED]** Only non-CAS calculators are allowed; the TI-84 Plus CE Python has no CAS, so the
device itself is acceptable.

**[VERIFIED]** Bluebook embeds a Desmos calculator with graphing and scientific modes, available for
the entire Math section; students can toggle between them at any point. Calculators are not allowed
on the Reading and Writing section and must be placed under the desk during it.

**[VERIFIED]** College Board explicitly acknowledges the AP/SAT divergence, in its own FAQ:

> "Why are calculators with CAS functionality allowed on AP exams but not allowed on the SAT Suite?
> Calculators with computer algebra system (CAS) functionality can affect accurate assessment of the
> math abilities that the SAT Suite is designed to test. CAS functionality doesn't present the same
> problem for AP exams."

This is a useful, citable confirmation that AP and SAT rules genuinely differ — which supports
making an AP-specific claim without it bleeding into an SAT claim.

**[VERIFIED]** SAT Suite calculator guidelines "may change as frequently as once a year," finalized
before each August SAT administration.

## 3. ACT (and ACT WorkKeys Applied Math)

### 3.1 Primary sources

- *ACT Calculator Policy* (PDF, marked "UPDATED: June 1, 2026") —
  <https://www.act.org/content/dam/act/unsecured/documents/ACT-calculator-policy.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *ACT Calculator Policy* (web) —
  <https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/test-day/calculator-policy.html> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *CAS Calculator FAQ* —
  <https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/test-day/cas-faq.html> (accessed 2026-08-12)

### 3.2 The device is fine; the programs are the problem

**[VERIFIED]** The ACT works from a prohibited list. The TI-84 family is not on it. Prohibited are
CAS calculators (TI-89/TI-92 model families, TI-Nspire CAS, HP Prime, HP 48GII, HP 40G/49G/50G
families, Casio ClassPad family, Algebra fx 2.0, CFX-9970G family), handheld/tablet/laptop
computers, pen-input devices, phone calculators, and "Calculators with QWERTY format letter keys."

**[VERIFIED]** ACT Calculator Policy, "permitted, but only after they are modified as noted":

> "Calculators that can hold programs or documents: Remove all documents and programs that have
> computer algebra system functionality."

**[VERIFIED]** CAS FAQ:

> "Only programs that have CAS functionality must be removed. All documents, however, must be
> removed regardless of their purpose or function."

**[VERIFIED]** CAS FAQ — Press-to-Test is explicitly rejected as a compliance method:

> "ACT's calculator policy calls for the programs to be removed. Disabling programs – for example,
> putting the calculator in Press to Test mode – is not enough."

**[VERIFIED]** CAS FAQ — the rules that *permitted* math programs must satisfy:

> "The kinds of mathematics-related programs permitted for the ACT allow students to use the
> calculator capabilities to do the routine calculations yet require students to show their analysis
> skills by choosing the right operations and process. Mathematics-related programs are allowed if
> they are single-purpose – for example, finding numeric solutions to a quadratic equation."

> "Programs must be written in the language of the calculator. Programs written in a different
> language, compiled, and loaded onto the calculator are not allowed. In this way, the programs can
> be examined on the calculator and will use calculator functions. Programs must not be lengthy. The
> limit is 25 logical lines of code. Programs cannot call another program. Techniques such as
> compressing code into a data statement are not allowed as a way of getting around this limit."

### 3.3 Analysis — this is the finding that most constrains ACT marketing

**[INFERRED]** Three of ACT's four conditions are a problem for this product:

1. **The 25-logical-line limit.** Essentially every program in this repository exceeds 25 logical
   lines. Under a plain reading, they are not permitted programs for the ACT, *even though they
   contain no CAS functionality.*
2. **Single-purpose.** Several programs are menu-driven multi-tools (`matrix_toolkit.py`,
   `unit_converter.py`, `ohms_law_circuits.py`, `vector3d_toolkit.py`,
   `combinatorics_probability.py`, `ideal_gas_law.py`, `acid_base_calculator.py`,
   `exam_countdown_drill.py`). A menu offering several distinct operations is the opposite of
   "single-purpose."
3. **`formula_flashcards.py`** is a stored formula reference, which is precisely what ACT's
   "remove all documents … regardless of their purpose or function" rule is aimed at, and it also
   fails the "requires students to show their analysis skills" rationale.

**[UNVERIFIED]** Whether on-device Python counts as "the language of the calculator" for a TI-84
Plus CE Python. The argument in favour is decent: TI ships Python as a first-class on-device
language, the source is stored as inspectable text on the calculator, and it uses the calculator's
own runtime rather than being cross-compiled elsewhere — which satisfies ACT's stated *reason* for
the rule ("the programs can be examined on the calculator and will use calculator functions"). The
argument against is that the FAQ was plainly written for TI-BASIC-era programs. **ACT has published
no TI-84 Python-specific guidance that I could find.** Do not rely on either reading.

**[INFERRED]** Net verdict: **do not market these programs for use during the ACT.** The 25-line
limit alone is disqualifying for nearly the whole library, and it does not depend on resolving the
Python-language question.

### 3.4 Other ACT facts

**[VERIFIED]** "Calculators may only be used on the mathematics test, including ACT® WorkKeys®
Applied Math." So WorkKeys Applied Math inherits the same calculator policy and the same program
restrictions.

**[VERIFIED]** "Examinees found not following this policy may be dismissed and their tests voided
for prohibited behavior."

**[VERIFIED]** In computer-based testing, "The Desmos on-screen calculator will be available," and
"The Desmos basic four-function calculator is available for WorkKeys."

**[VERIFIED]** ACT's own posture on ambiguity, from the CAS FAQ:

> "Given the proliferation of math resources/tools, ACT is not fully familiar with all mathematics
> programs and apps that are available for use on a calculator. If you are unsure whether a program
> or app is prohibited, our recommendation is to not install it or remove it before test day."

## 4. NCEES — FE, PE, FS, PS engineering and surveying licensure exams

### 4.1 Primary sources

- NCEES *Exams* page, Calculator Policy section — <https://ncees.org/exams/> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- NCEES, *Notice of Future Changes to NCEES Exams and Supporting Materials*, memo from Lehmon Dekle,
  P.E., Chief Officer of Examinations, dated **October 20, 2025** —
  <https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Fall-2026-Exam-Changes-Member-Board-Memo.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- NCEES *Examinee Guide*, May 2026 —
  <https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NCEESExamGuide_May-2026.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-12)

### 4.2 The rule

**[VERIFIED]** NCEES calculator policy, on ncees.org:

> "The following calculator models are the only ones acceptable for use during the 2026 exams:"
>
> - "**Casio:** All fx-115 and fx-991 models (Any Casio calculator must have "fx-115" or "fx-991" in
>   its model name.)"
> - "**Texas Instruments:** All TI-30X and TI-36X models (Any Texas Instruments calculator must have
>   "TI-30X" or "TI-36X" in its model name.)"
> - Hewlett Packard: HP 33s and HP 35s models

**[VERIFIED]** The October 2025 NCEES member-board memo confirms the same list for 2026 and states
there is no change:

> "2026 Approved Calculators
> There will be no change to the approved calculators for 2026. The approved calculator list for
> 2026 NCEES examinations is as follows:
> • Hewlett-Packard: HP 33s
> • Hewlett-Packard: HP 35s
> • Casio: fx-115 models (i.e., Casio models beginning with "fx-115")
> • Casio: fx-991 models (i.e., Casio models beginning with "fx-991")
> • Texas Instruments: TI-30X models (i.e., TI models beginning with "TI-30X")
> • Texas Instruments: TI-36X models (i.e., TI models beginning with "TI-36X")"

**[VERIFIED]** NCEES Examinee Guide, May 2026:

> "You are allowed to bring one NCEES-approved calculator into the testing room. You may store spare
> calculators and covers with your personal belongings. … A TI-30XS calculator can be accessed for
> on-screen use during the exam."

### 4.3 Verdict

**[VERIFIED, by direct logical application of the quoted rule]** NCEES's TI rule is a **model-name
string test**: the model name must contain "TI-30X" or "TI-36X." "TI-84 Plus CE Python" contains
neither. **The TI-84 Plus CE Python is prohibited on the FE, PE, FS, and PS exams. There is no
exception, no exam mode that fixes it, and no discipline for which it is allowed.** A candidate who
brings one will be told to take the exam without a calculator.

**Marketing consequence:** any statement, tag, keyword, or listing implying these programs are
usable on the FE or PE exam would be a straightforwardly false statement of fact about a third
party's published rule. Do not do it, in any form, ever — including SEO keywords like "FE exam
calculator."

**[INFERRED]** There remains one honest and quite narrow adjacent use: an engineering *student*
taking coursework (statics, circuits, thermo) may well be allowed to use a TI-84 in class. That is
a coursework claim, not a licensure-exam claim, and it must be phrased so the two cannot be
confused.

## 5. IB Diploma Programme

### 5.1 Sources — read the caveat

- *Use of calculators in IB DP examinations 2026 – version 1.0* —
  <https://media-acs.zentech.gr/filesystem/Multimedia/pdf/Use_of_Calculators_2026_id962.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- *Conduct of examinations booklet 2026* —
  <https://media-acs.zentech.gr/filesystem/Multimedia/pdf/ib_Conduct_of_Examinations_Booklet_2026_id947.pdf> (accessed 2026-08-12)
- IB news item, *IB enhances assessment procedures for November 2024 exams* —
  <https://www.ibo.org/news/news-about-the-ib/ib-enhances-assessment-procedures-for-november-2024-exams/> (accessed 2026-08-12)

**[UNVERIFIED — source provenance]** The two IB booklets above are authentic IB documents, but the
copies I could reach are hosted on a **third-party school mirror**, not on ibo.org. The
authoritative copies live behind the IB Programme Resource Centre, which requires school
credentials. I found the same operative text in the 2024, 2025, and 2026 versions across
independently hosted mirrors, which raises my confidence considerably, but I cannot certify it
against ibo.org. **Treat the exact wording as high-confidence-but-unconfirmed. Do not quote it in
marketing.** The one piece of IB text that *is* on ibo.org is the news item confirming that schools
must clear calculator memories.

### 5.2 What the documents say

**[VERIFIED, subject to the provenance caveat]** *Use of calculators in IB DP examinations 2026*:

> "A calculator will not be permitted for IB DP examinations if: … it includes additional elements,
> for example third-party applications or student generated notes, which are not removed (via a
> reset) or blocked (via an examination mode)."

> "Students may not use or store data/notes, programs or flash (ROM) applications (Apps) in their
> calculators that may assist them in an examination by removing the need to recall facts or
> formulae."

**[VERIFIED, subject to the provenance caveat]** *Conduct of examinations booklet 2026*, §2.5:

> "In addition to checking calculators in advance of an examination, there is a requirement to clear
> the memories of all calculators at the end of each examination. This is so students cannot take
> notes made on the calculator out of the examination. This action should include resetting the RAM
> and ROM memory where applicable."

**[VERIFIED — on ibo.org]** IB news item:

> "Finally, schools are now required to clear the memories of all calculators. While the IB
> encourages the use of technology like Graphical Display Calculators, schools will be required to
> clear the devices' memory at the end of each exam."

### 5.3 Verdict

**[INFERRED]** The TI-84 Plus (including CE and Python versions) is a permitted non-CAS device for
IB. But IB is the *most* explicit of any authority reviewed here that third-party programs and
stored formula references must be removed or blocked. `formula_flashcards.py` is exactly the thing
the "removing the need to recall facts or formulae" clause names. **IB: study-only. Never claim
in-exam use.**

**[INFERRED]** There *is* an honest CLEP angle, but it is a **familiarity** angle, not a program

students who already know the TI-84 workflow have an advantage. You may say that. You may not imply
any program can be loaded.

### 6.2 GED

**[VERIFIED]** GED Testing Service, *Take the GED Test Online*
(<https://www.ged.com/take-the-ged-test-online.html>, accessed 2026-08-12):

> "You will not be able to use a physical calculator but will have access to an onscreen calculator
> during the exam."

**[VERIFIED]** GED's own blog states the calculator used is the TI-30XS MultiView and that "This
calculator comes with your test and is displayed as a digital version, so there's no need to buy and
bring this device with you."
(<https://www.ged.com/blog/can-you-use-a-calculator-on-the-ged-math-test.html>)

**[UNVERIFIED]** Whether some in-person GED test centers permit a candidate's own handheld TI-30XS.
Third-party sources disagree on this. It does not matter for us either way: no GED pathway permits a
graphing calculator. **Do not market to GED.**

### 6.3 ACT WorkKeys Applied Math

**[VERIFIED]** Covered by the ACT Calculator Policy quoted in §3: calculators may be used on
WorkKeys Applied Math, subject to the same prohibited list and the same program/document removal
rules, and a Desmos four-function calculator is provided in CBT. Same verdict as the ACT.

### 6.4 State standardized tests

**[UNVERIFIED — and deliberately not researched exhaustively]** State testing programs set their own
policies and there are 50+ of them. As one concrete illustration of how varied these are: Alberta
Education's *Calculator Information and Rules 2025-2026*
(<https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/edc-calculator-information-and-rules.pdf>,
accessed 2026-08-12) lists the TI-84 Plus CE Python as an approved model and notes that
"Press-to-Test or Test Guard can be used rather than the directions provided in the table for some
of these calculators in order to preserve stored programs, applications, and other data if desired."
That is one jurisdiction, not a general rule. **Never make a state-testing claim without checking
that specific state.**

## 7. University and course-level exams

**[INFERRED — no primary authority exists to cite, and that is precisely the point]** There is no
central body governing classroom exam rules. Individual instructors, departments, and academic
integrity offices set them, and they vary wildly: some allow anything on the calculator, many
require a memory reset or Press-to-Test at the door, many have a blanket "no programs" rule, and
some prohibit programmable calculators entirely.

The only defensible marketing posture is: **this is the buyer's responsibility, and the product's
job is to make compliance easy** — which means shipping clear backup/restore instructions so a
student can strip and restore the calculator around an exam without losing their purchase.

### 7.1 Press-to-Test and Python programs — the critical technical finding

This matters as much for product support as for compliance.

**[VERIFIED]** TI-84 Plus CE Python eGuide, *Setting Up Test Mode*
(<https://education.ti.com/html/eguides/graphing/84pluscepy/en/content/eg_gsguide/m_exams/exm_set_test_mode.HTML>,
accessed 2026-08-12), under "Note":

> "Pic & Image Vars are disabled."
> "**All variables stored in RAM and in archived memory are deleted.**"
> "In memory management (2nd MEM, 2:Mem Management/Delete...), disabled files will display with the
> not equal sign."

**[VERIFIED]** TI *Press-to-Test Guidebook*
(<https://education.ti.com/~/media/FA881349A68545328C98B49B86A64D90>, accessed 2026-08-12), in the
TI-84 Plus mode section:

> "Other variables stored in RAM and in archived memory (**including AppVars**) are deleted."

**[VERIFIED]** TI-84 Plus CE Python eGuide, *Using Python App*
(<https://education.ti.com/html/webhelp/eg_ti84pluscepy/en/content/eg_pythonappprog/m_pyadpapp/m_pyappuse.HTML>,
accessed 2026-08-12):

> "Python programs stored or created as Python AppVars will execute from RAM. You may store Python
> AppVars in Archive for memory management…"

**[VERIFIED]** TI Knowledge Base Solution 34886
(<https://education.ti.com/en/customer-support/knowledge-base/ti-83-84-plus-family/product-usage/34886>,
accessed 2026-08-12):

> "The main difference is that the Press-to-Test function temporarily **disables** flash
> applications, rather than deleting them. After the exam, applications and programs can be restored
> easily for classroom use."

**[INFERRED — this is the important conclusion]** Press-to-Test treats different file types
differently, and the popular understanding ("Press-to-Test just hides everything") is wrong for
Python:

| File type | What Press-to-Test does |
|---|---|
| Flash Apps (including the Python App itself) | **Disabled**, not deleted; restored on exit |
| TI-BASIC programs (`prgm`) | **Disabled**, not deleted; restored on exit |
| Pic / Image Vars | **Disabled** |
| **AppVars — which is how Python programs are stored** | **Deleted** |

Chaining TI's own two statements — "variables stored in RAM and archived memory (including AppVars)
are deleted" and "Python programs [are] stored or created as Python AppVars" — the conclusion is
that **entering Press-to-Test destroys the customer's Python programs.** They are not hidden and
they do not come back when exam mode is exited.

**[UNVERIFIED]** TI does not, anywhere I could find, write the sentence "Python AppVars are deleted
by Press-to-Test" in those exact words on a CE Python-specific page. The conclusion is a two-step
inference from TI documents, so I am labelling it honestly. **But the practical instruction is the
same either way, and the owner should give it unconditionally:** *back up your programs to a
computer with TI Connect™ CE before entering Press-to-Test, and re-send them afterward.* That
instruction is correct whether the files are deleted or merely blocked, and it converts a potential
support disaster and refund magnet into a documented, handled workflow.

**[VERIFIED]** Related, and also worth telling buyers: TI's *Exam Prep Guide*
(<https://education.ti.com/en/resources/test-preparation/education-test-prep-guides/ti-84-plus-series>,
accessed 2026-08-12) states that a full memory reset "permanently deletes all user-installed
applications from the calculator" and advises backing up first. Press-to-Test is exited by
transferring any file to the calculator from another CE unit, or via TI Connect™ CE →
Actions → Quit Exam Mode on Connected CE Calculators.

## 8. Legal, liability, and platform considerations for the seller

> This section is research and commentary, not legal advice. For anything with real money at stake,
> get a lawyer.

### 8.1 Is selling this lawful?

**[INFERRED]** Yes, plainly. These are original works of software authorship, sold as educational
tools. There is an established market for exactly this (mcstutoring.com being the obvious
comparator, plus the long-standing TI-BASIC program marketplace). Nothing about the product category
is restricted. The risk in this business is not *whether* you may sell it — it is *what you say
about it.*

### 8.2 The real exposure: advertising accuracy

**[VERIFIED]** FTC, *Advertising and Marketing* (<https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing>,
accessed 2026-08-12):

> "Under the law, claims in advertisements must be truthful, cannot be deceptive or unfair, and must
> be evidence-based."

**[INFERRED]** "AP-Exam-Legal" / "AP-approved" is an **objective factual claim about a third party's
published policy**, not puffery. Three things follow:

1. If the claim is wrong for even one of the exams a reasonable buyer would read it to cover, it is
   deceptive. "AP-approved" reads to a buyer as *College Board has approved these programs.* College
   Board has approved the *device*. It has approved no third party's programs, and it maintains no
   program-approval process at all. **That claim is false as written, even though the underlying
   policy is permissive.**
2. The claim must be *substantiated at the time it is made* and kept current. Policies change
   annually; a claim that was true in 2024 and is unrevised in 2026 is still your problem.
3. Realistic downside is mostly commercial rather than regulatory at this scale — chargebacks,
   refunds, marketplace takedowns, a public complaint from a student who got flagged — but state
   consumer-protection (UDAP) statutes generally allow private suits, and "I bought this because it
   said it was exam-approved and I was dismissed from my exam" is exactly the fact pattern those
   statutes exist for.

**[INFERRED]** Note the asymmetry that makes accuracy cheap here: the *true* AP story
("College Board says you don't need to clear your calculator's memory, and the TI-84 Plus CE Python
is on its approved list") is nearly as commercially strong as the false one, and it comes with a
quotable source. There is very little to gain from overclaiming and a lot to lose.

### 8.3 Academic-integrity facilitation

**[INFERRED]** There is no general US law against selling a study tool that a buyer might misuse. The
risk is framing. Marketing that says "beat the exam," "sneak formulas past your proctor," "the
answers your teacher doesn't want you to have," or that foregrounds concealment, converts a
legitimate educational product into something that looks like cheating facilitation — which is what
attracts platform enforcement, school complaints, press, and (in the worst case) claims of tortious
interference or unfair competition from a testing body.

Concretely: **the marketing must never advertise the value of *hiding* the programs, or of using
them where they are not allowed.** Sell time saved on homework, error-checking on practice problems,
and faster, more confident work in exams that do permit them. Those are real, and they are safe.

Note also **[VERIFIED]** that College Board bans individuals — not just cancels scores — for
integrity violations: "College Board will prohibit individuals from taking the SAT, AP, or CLEP
exams when we conclude they've deliberately gained or attempted to gain or share an unfair advantage
on any College Board test" (*Exam Security Policies*, accessed 2026-08-12). Your buyers are students
with real stakes. Getting one of them banned because your copy told them something wrong is the
actual worst case, and it is worse than any fine.

### 8.4 Platform policies

**Gumroad.** **[VERIFIED]** Gumroad's prohibited products list
(<https://gumroad.com/prohibited>, accessed 2026-08-12) does **not** include educational software or
study tools. Study software is squarely allowed. Four nearby entries are worth reading as boundary
markers: "essay mills/paper mills," "hacking and cracking materials which includes manuals, how-to
guides, information, or equipment enabling illegal access to software, servers, website, or other
protected property," "deceptive marketing practices," and "copyrighted media and software which
includes unauthorized copies…". **[INFERRED]** None applies to original study programs. The one that
could plausibly be triggered by bad copy is "deceptive marketing practices" — another reason to keep
claims accurate.

**Etsy.** **[VERIFIED]** Etsy Seller Policy (<https://www.etsy.com/legal/sellers/>, accessed
2026-08-12, version effective July 9, 2026):

> "everything listed for sale on Etsy must be made, designed, handpicked, or sourced by a seller."

**[VERIFIED]** Etsy Help, *What Can I Sell on Etsy?*
(<https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-in/articles/360024112614-What-Can-I-Sell-on-Etsy>, accessed
2026-08-12):

> "Digital items listed in your shop must be made and/or designed by you, the seller."

**[INFERRED]** The digital bundles are fine — they are original code written by the seller, which is
the cleanest possible fit for "made and/or designed by you." Two cautions:

- **[INFERRED]** The **pre-loaded physical calculator** idea is materially riskier on Etsy than the
  digital bundles. A TI-84 is a mass-produced third-party device; Etsy restricts reselling
  mass-produced items and the "handpicked/sourced" categories have their own criteria. **[UNVERIFIED]**
  I did not confirm how Etsy's current Creativity Standards would classify "mass-produced device +
  seller's own software preloaded." Check this before listing, and consider selling preloaded
  hardware on Gumroad or a direct storefront instead, where the question doesn't arise.
- **[VERIFIED]** Etsy's IP policy is complaint-driven: "If Etsy receives proper notice of
  intellectual property infringement, we will comply with our policy and remove the specified
  material." Trademark-sloppy listing titles are the most likely trigger for a takedown, and
  takedowns on Etsy are cheap for the complainant and expensive for you.

### 8.5 Trademarks — this is the most concretely actionable legal section

#### College Board (AP®, SAT®, PSAT/NMSQT®, CLEP®, Advanced Placement®)

**[VERIFIED]** *Guidelines for Using College Board Trademarks*
(<https://privacy.collegeboard.org/copyright-trademark/guidelines>, accessed 2026-08-12). The
operative rules for a product listing:

> "You must use College Board trademarks as adjectives. … As adjectives, trademarks may not be used
> in the plural or possessive form."
> Correct: "SAT® exams" or "Advanced Placement® course". Incorrect: "Improve Your SATs" or "Review
> AP's Curriculum".

> "Titles. Do not use College Board trademarks in the main title of your product, service, or
> publication, without identifying the source or owner.
> **Incorrect Use: "AP Calculus Guide" or "SAT Preparation and Practice App."**
> **Correct Use: "John Doe's Guide to AP® Calculus" or "Smith's App for the SAT® Exam"**"

> "Combined Marks. Do not combine College Board trademarks with any other trademark or design,
> (e.g., Google's SAT Word of the Day) or use the marks in a manner which suggests ownership by a
> third party (e.g., ACME's CLEP Study Guide)."

> "Company, Product, Service and Domain Names. Do not use or register, in whole or in part, any
> College Board trademark, logo, or alteration thereof, as part of company names, company taglines
> or slogans, social media pages, Internet domain names, web site addresses, or **meta tags**."

> "Appearance. Do not over-emphasize the College Board mark or use it more prominently than your
> product/service name. The College Board mark must be smaller in font, size, and color compared to
> your product/service name…"

> "The College Board DOES NOT ALLOW third parties to leverage its brand, reputation and trademarks
> in search engine marketing and advertisements, such as Google AdWords, Facebook, etc."

The required disclaimer template, verbatim:

> "[Trademark® or Trademark™] is a trademark [registered / owned] by the College Board, which is not
> affiliated with, and does not endorse, this [product/site]."

With a mandatory variant for PSAT/NMSQT:

> "PSAT/NMSQT® is a registered trademark of the College Board and the National Merit Scholarship
> Corporation, which are not affiliated with, and do not endorse, this [product/site]."

And placement requirements: on packaging, labels, advertisements and promotional material "the
footnote/disclaimer must appear on the same surface as the College Board mark"; for websites, "on
the web site home page and on all internal web pages that include College Board marks," and
"Merely stating trademark ownership credit and/or including the endorsement disclaimer in the
'Terms and Conditions' or 'Legal Notice' sections of a web site does NOT satisfy these Guidelines."

**[VERIFIED]** College Board's stated position on fair use is narrow:

> "'Fair use' of a trademark is allowed without express permission when a third party simply refers
> to the College Board product or service associated with the trademark. … Using a College Board
> trademark in association with a consumer product or service without express permission is not
> considered fair use. For example, an advertisement for an SAT® tutoring company is not allowed
> under fair use."

**[INFERRED — and be honest with yourself about this]** That last sentence is College Board's
*position*, not settled law. US trademark doctrine recognises nominative fair use (broadly: you may
use a mark to refer to the trademark owner's own product where you can't reasonably identify it
otherwise, you use no more of the mark than necessary, and you don't suggest sponsorship). A great
deal of legitimate test-prep publishing exists on exactly that basis. But College Board's guidelines
are what its enforcement team applies, and a marketplace takedown is decided by a marketplace, not a
court. **The commercially sensible move is to comply with College Board's stated guidelines even
where the law might permit more.** That costs almost nothing and removes an entire category of risk.

**[VERIFIED — useful precedent]** Texas Instruments, a sophisticated party with lawyers, uses
exactly this belt-and-braces approach in its own materials. Its branding glossary
(<https://education.ti.com/en/branding/glossary-of-terms>, accessed 2026-08-12) prescribes:

> "AP® is a trademark registered by the College Board, which is not affiliated with, and does not
> endorse, TI products. Policies subject to change. Visit www.collegeboard.org."

The "Policies subject to change. Visit www.collegeboard.org." tail is a smart addition worth copying.

#### ACT

**[VERIFIED]** ACT *Terms of Use* (<https://www.act.org/content/act/en/terms-of-use.html>, accessed
2026-08-12) lists ACT®, WorkKeys®, PreACT®, and others as ACT marks, and states:

> "No use of any ACT trademark is permitted without express written consent from ACT. For more
> information, contact the ACT Publications Department at Publications@act.org."

> "No permission is given to use the official ACT logo or any other graphics or images from the
> Site."

**[UNVERIFIED]** I could not find a public, third-party-facing ACT trademark *usage guidelines*
document comparable to College Board's — only the blanket Terms of Use prohibition and references to
"ACT's Trademark Use Guidelines" inside ACT's institutional contracts. **[INFERRED]** Given that ACT
is stricter on paper than College Board and that these programs are not ACT-compliant anyway (§3),
the right answer is simple: **don't put "ACT" in product titles, listing titles, or keywords at
all.** There is no upside.

#### Texas Instruments

**[VERIFIED]** TI's own recommended generic third-party disclaimer
(<https://education.ti.com/en/branding/ti-trademarks-and-brands>, accessed 2026-08-12):

> "All (registered) trademarks are the property of their respective owners and are not affiliated
> with, nor endorsed by Texas Instruments."

**[VERIFIED]** TI Terms of Use (<https://www.ti.com/legal/terms-conditions/terms-of-use.html>,
accessed 2026-08-12) permits plain-text links to TI sites provided the link does not "use any of
TI's copyrighted information or logos, designs, slogans, product trademarks or service marks,"
"present false or misleading information or impressions about TI products or services," or "suggest
that TI promotes, endorses, or has any relationship with any third party…"

**[INFERRED]** Using "TI-84 Plus CE Python" to state what hardware the software runs on is
compatibility/nominative use and is normal, defensible, and unavoidable — you literally cannot
describe the product otherwise. The rules to follow: use the full accurate model name, use it as an
adjective describing compatibility ("for the TI-84 Plus CE Python Edition"), never use TI logos or
product images, never build "TI" or "TI-84" into a brand name or domain, and carry the
non-affiliation footer. **[UNVERIFIED]** I found no TI trademark usage guideline document written
specifically for calculator-accessory third parties (the detailed public one,
<https://www.ti.com/lit/dlpt010>, is DLP-specific); its principles — no logos, no marks in company
or product names, no implied endorsement, no slogans, no confusingly similar domains — are the
standard TI posture and are safe to follow by analogy, but that extension is my inference.
