Suggested USD pricing for the individual programs, subject bundles, and the complete
20+ program toolkit, grounded in current competitor and general digital-download pricing
research (see "Market Research" below).

pricing 10–20% lower per the marketplace's more price-sensitive, impulse-buy audience (see
below), e.g. $12 subject bundles / $30 complete toolkit.

## Rationale

- **Free starter bundle → paid funnel.** The free bundle mirrors the exact funnel pattern
  used by mcstutoring.com ("Free TI-84 Starter Bundle: five essential programs... completely
  free") and is standard practice for digital-download sellers: it costs nothing to
  distribute, builds trust/reviews, demonstrates program quality, and is the natural
  on-ramp to a paid subject bundle. Our 3-program version (a unit converter, quadratic
  solver, and descriptive-stats tool) was chosen because these are useful across *many*
  courses, not just one subject — maximizing the audience who'll try it and come back.
- **Subject bundles priced as the "sweet spot" impulse/considered purchase.** At $14 each
  for 6 programs (~$2.33/program), subject bundles sit comfortably inside the "bundle /
  template" range ($9–$25 on Gumroad, $5–$20 on Etsy) that general digital-download pricing
  research shows converts well without needing an established audience or reviews yet.
  They're priced well under mcstutoring.com's exam-specific solver bundles ($40–$75 each),
  which is appropriate: those are marketed as full AP-curriculum "solvers" with heavier unit
  coverage, while ours are general-purpose calculation tools with an honest (non-"AP-legal")
  disclaimer — a slightly lower price reflects the narrower, more literal scope and sets
  accurate buyer expectations.
- **Complete Toolkit priced below the sum of its parts, on purpose.** This is the same
  "bundle discount" logic mcstutoring.com uses across the board (e.g. their AP Physics
  Ultimate Bundle is marketed as "$205 value" for $100; their Chemistry Complete Bundle
  as "$150 value" for $50). Pricing the Complete Toolkit at $35 — well under the $56 sum of
  the 4 subject bundles — gives price-sensitive buyers (e.g. students taking multiple STEM
  classes, or just wanting "everything") a clear incentive to buy the bigger bundle instead
  of piecing together 2+ subject bundles, which increases average order value even though
  the per-unit price drops.
- **Individual program price ($3–$4)** exists mainly as a price anchor that makes the
  bundles look obviously better value (6 programs for $14 vs. $18–24 à la carte) — most
  sellers in this space report the bulk of revenue comes from bundle purchases, not
  singles, and mcstutoring.com's own "Bundle Value Breakdown" tables use the same
  anchoring technique.

Their model: heavily curriculum-branded ("Units 1–8," "AP-Exam-Legal" marketing), higher
price points per bundle ($40–$160), value-anchored against a much higher struck-through
"if bought separately" price, and a free 5-program starter bundle as the top-of-funnel
lead magnet.

**General digital-download / study-tool pricing (Etsy & Gumroad, 2026):**

- Etsy: short guides/tools $3–$15; bundles of multiple items $15–$25; buyers expect
  impulse-buy pricing (Etsy takes ~6.5% + $0.20 listing + card processing).
- Gumroad: short guides/tools $5–$19; bundles $15–$40; premium/specialized tools $29–$97;
  flat 10% fee, no listing fee — better suited to higher-ticket, direct-audience sales.
- Consensus guidance across both platforms: avoid pricing under ~$9 on a per-listing basis
  once you're past the free/lead-magnet tier, since flat fees eat a large share of very
  low-priced sales; bundle 2–3 related items to justify a $15–$25 price point.

**Conclusion:** our subject-bundle price of $14 and complete-toolkit price of $35 sit inside
the observed ranges for both the direct competitor (scaled down to reflect narrower,
non-curriculum-specific scope and an honest non-"legal-claim" disclaimer) and general
digital study-tool pricing norms on Etsy/Gumroad.
