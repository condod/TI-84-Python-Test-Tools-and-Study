# Launch & Traffic Plan

How to actually get buyers for the TI-84 Plus CE Python toolkit, channel by channel, with
honest effort and impact estimates and a two-week launch sequence.

**On sourcing:** claims below are marked **VERIFIED** (I read the policy text myself and the
URL is given), **SECONDARY** (a third party reports it; I could not read the original), or
**INFERRED** (my reasoning, no source). Reddit blocks automated access to its rules pages —
every fetch of `reddit.com/r/*/about/rules` returned HTTP 403 — so **no subreddit rule text
below is verified**, and §2 gives you a five-minute procedure to check each one yourself
before you post. Do not skip it. Getting this wrong costs you the account you'd be building
on.

---

## 1. Start from an honest baseline

You have: a real product, a free lead magnet, zero audience, zero reviews, zero traffic, and
a competitor (mcstutoring.com) already occupying the "TI-84 program bundles" position at
2–5× your price.

You do not have: an existing following to launch to. That single fact determines everything
below. Channels that convert an existing audience (email blasts, Gumroad Discover, paid ads)
are worthless to you right now. Channels that reach strangers who are already looking for
this — marketplace search, YouTube search, community answers — are the entire game.

**What realistic success looks like in the first 60 days:** a handful of sales, a few dozen
free-pack downloads, and one channel that shows a pulse. The goal of the launch is not
revenue, it is *finding which single channel works* and then doing far more of it. Judge the
launch on that, not on the dollar figure.

**The three highest-leverage channels, in order:**

1. **YouTube search + Etsy search** — the only two places where strangers actively type what
   you sell. Slow to start, compounds indefinitely, zero ban risk.
2. **Answering questions in communities** (Reddit comments, Cemetech threads, Discord help
   channels) — near-zero risk when done as a genuine answer, and it reaches people at the
   exact moment of need.
3. **Short-form video (TikTok / Shorts / Reels)** — the only channel with real upside
   variance. Most posts do nothing; one can do 200k views. Cheap to attempt.

Everything else is a supporting act.

## 2. Verify every subreddit's rules yourself — five-minute procedure

I could not retrieve these programmatically. You can retrieve them in a browser in seconds,
and you must, because the penalty for guessing is a ban from the exact community you need.

For each subreddit, before posting anything:

1. Open `https://old.reddit.com/r/NAME/` — the old interface shows the **full sidebar rules**,
   which the redesign truncates. Read all of them, not the short titles.
2. Open `https://www.reddit.com/r/NAME/about/rules/` for the expanded rule text.
3. Check the **wiki**: `https://www.reddit.com/r/NAME/wiki/index`. Detailed self-promotion
   policies usually live here rather than in the sidebar.
4. Look at the **pinned/stickied posts** at the top of the subreddit. Many communities corral
   all promotion into a recurring megathread, and that thread is the only legal place for it.
5. Search the subreddit for prior art: `site:reddit.com/r/NAME ti-84 programs`. If people have
   shared calculator programs there before and the posts are still up with upvotes, that is
   the strongest possible signal. If you find only removed posts, that is your answer.
6. If it is still ambiguous — **message the moderators and ask.** This is the single most
   underrated move available. Mods answer, a "yes" is durable permission, and a "no" costs you
   a post you would have had removed anyway.

Record what you find in a table like the one below so you never have to re-check.

**Additional near-universal constraints to plan around (SECONDARY/INFERRED):** most active
subreddits run AutoModerator filters on account age and karma (commonly in the range of days
to a month, and tens to hundreds of karma), and Reddit's long-standing informal 9:1 guideline
— roughly nine genuine contributions for every self-promotional one — is still how moderators
think even though Reddit has de-emphasised the exact wording. Do not create a fresh account
and immediately post a link. It will be filtered before a human sees it.

### 2.2 The strategy that works regardless of the rules

**Comment, don't post.** Roughly once a week, someone asks "how do I put programs on my
TI-84," "does the CE Python thing actually do anything," or "is there a program for
[topic]." Answering those completely — with the free link at the end, after the answer stands
on its own — is:

- allowed almost everywhere, because it's a genuine answer to a genuine question;
- higher-converting than any post, because the reader has stated their need;
- durable, because those threads rank in Google for years.

Draft B in `DEMO_SCRIPTS.md` is the template. Set up Reddit keyword alerts (or just search
`ti-84 python` sorted by new, weekly) so you catch these threads while they're live.

**Rules of engagement, non-negotiable:**

- One real account, used like a person. Comment on things you have no stake in.
- Never post the same text to two subreddits. Cross-posted identical copy is the clearest
  spam signal there is.
- Disclose that it's yours, every time. "I made these" costs nothing and prevents the
  accusation that actually kills accounts.
- Lead with the free thing. Mention paid once, at most, at the end.
- If a mod removes your post, do not repost it. Message them and ask what would be acceptable.
- Never argue in the comments about whether calculator programs are cheating. Restate the
  study-aid framing once, link the disclaimer, and disengage.

**Effort:** ~20 min/week. **Impact:** low volume, but the highest-quality traffic you'll get.
**Risk:** low, if you comment rather than post.

## 3. The calculator-enthusiast communities (Cemetech, ticalc.org, TI-Planet)

Different planet from the student subs. These are programmers who have been writing
calculator software since before your buyers were born, a strong free-software culture, and
low tolerance for advertising. They are also the only people who will meaningfully review
your code, and they carry outsized weight in what surfaces when someone Googles a TI-84
question.

**Treat them as a credibility and backlink channel, not a sales channel.**

### Cemetech (cemetech.net)

**VERIFIED** (from the live forum index at <https://www.cemetech.net/forum/>): the forum is
organised into topical sections including **Your Projects**, **Calculator Help**,
**TI-BASIC**, **z80 & ez80 Assembly**, **[TI] C and C++**, **Casio Development &
Programming**, **Using the TI-83 Plus/TI-84 Plus**, and **Technology & Calculator Open
Topic**. A project announcement belongs in **Your Projects**; answering someone's
CE-Python question belongs in **Calculator Help**.

**UNVERIFIED:** I could not retrieve Cemetech's forum rules or an explicit policy on
commercial software. **Read the posting rules and the FAQ before your first post**, and read
a few weeks of *Your Projects* threads to calibrate tone. Cemetech also runs a live shoutbox
("SAX") and, by widespread report, a bridged Discord — verify both from the site's own
navigation rather than taking my word for it.

**How to post there:** Draft C in `DEMO_SCRIPTS.md`. Lead with the engineering constraints you
worked under (no `cmath`, the AppVar storage model, the 100-element list cap, `ti_plotlib`
guarding), link the GitHub repo — which is genuinely open — and mention the paid bundles once
in a single sentence that acknowledges the community's preference for free software. Then
stay and answer technical criticism. A one-and-done promo post is remembered for years.

**Effort:** one good post + ongoing participation. **Impact:** low direct sales, meaningful
credibility and links. **Risk:** low if you lead with substance, reputational if you don't.

### ticalc.org

**VERIFIED** — this one has explicit, quotable published rules, and one of them matters
enormously to you.

From ticalc.org's file-archive FAQ (<https://ticalc.org/help/faq/42.html>), the archive does
not accept files that, among other things:

> - were not written by the uploader (unless the original author notifies us in writing that said uploader has permission to upload the file)
> - would be trivial for someone familiar with the basics of calculator programming to write
> - do nothing more than duplicate a built-in function or feature of the calculator
> - **are intended primarily to facilitate cheating on school assignments or tests (e.g., fake RAM-clearing programs)**
> - are otherwise deemed to have little or no use, as determined by the file archiver(s)

and it states: *"We reserve the right to refuse any submitted file for any reason at our sole
discretion."* Submission requires a free account and goes through the upload form
(<https://ticalc.org/archives/files/contributing.html>).

**Read those criteria as product feedback, not just as rules.** Three of them bear on you
directly: your positioning must not read as a cheating aid (it doesn't, if you keep the
disclaimer), a bare quadratic solver risks "trivial to write" or "duplicates a built-in
feature," and everything must be your own code.

**What to do:** submit the **free starter pack** — or better, two or three of the
*substantial* programs (the ODE solver, the RLC impedance tool, the 3D vector toolkit; not
the unit converter) — as free archive entries, with the GitHub repo linked in the readme.
Do not attempt to list the paid bundles there.

**UNVERIFIED:** whether ticalc.org still maintains a shareware/commercial category. Nothing in
the acceptance criteria bans commercial software outright, but the archive's culture is
overwhelmingly free. Don't push it.

**Effort:** an hour to prepare a submission. **Impact:** a permanent, high-authority backlink
and a trickle of traffic for years. **Risk:** essentially zero if you submit free files.

### TI-Planet (tiplanet.org)

Large, active, French-language-primary TI community with its own archive and news feed. Same
norms as the above: free software culture, project posts welcome, advertising not. **Fully
UNVERIFIED** — I did not fetch their rules. Worth an hour of reading before deciding. Their
news feed covers new CE Python projects, which is exactly your category.

## 4. Short-form video (TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels)

The only channel with genuine upside variance, and the most under-served: the "TI-84 does
things you didn't know about" genre reliably performs, and almost nobody is making it for the
Python Edition specifically.

Five scripts, shot-ready, are in `DEMO_SCRIPTS.md`.

**How to run it:**

- Post the **same video to all three platforms**. The marginal cost is minutes and the
  algorithms are independent.
- **3–5 posts a week for a month** before drawing any conclusions. Fewer than ~12 posts is not
  a test, it's a coin flip.
- Link the **free pack** in bio, never a paid product. Short-form traffic converts terribly to
  a paid page and fine to a free one.
- Reply to every comment in the first hour. It measurably affects distribution and it's where
  you'll learn what people actually want.
- Expect the hit rate to be roughly one in ten. Plan for it emotionally.

**What to avoid:** never frame a video as "get the answers," "beat the test," or anything with
a sneaking-past-the-teacher energy. That framing gets engagement from people who will never
buy, attracts hostile comments, and is exactly the association that makes your product
un-recommendable by the teachers and tutors in §7.

**Effort:** high — 2–4 h/week to do properly. **Impact:** highest ceiling, lowest floor.
**Risk:** none to your business, some to your patience.

### YouTube long-form is the sleeper

Script 5 in `DEMO_SCRIPTS.md` — the install tutorial — should also be published as a regular
5–8 minute YouTube video titled something like *How to Put Python Programs on a TI-84 Plus CE
(TI Connect CE, 2026)*.

This is the highest-value single asset in the entire plan. "How do I put programs on my TI-84"
is a permanent, seasonal, high-intent query; the existing videos are mostly old and about
TI-BASIC on monochrome models; and **every single viewer is by definition someone who wants
calculator programs.** Put the free pack link in the description and the pinned comment. One
video, made once, that collects qualified traffic for years.

**Effort:** one focused afternoon. **Impact:** the best effort-to-return ratio on this page.

## 5. Discord

**UNVERIFIED throughout** — I did not join or read the rules of any server below. Verify each
one's self-promotion policy (nearly all have a `#self-promo`, `#projects` or `#showcase`
channel, and posting outside it gets you banned fast).

Targets worth investigating, roughly in order of fit:

- **Cemetech's Discord** — believed to be bridged to the site's SAX shoutbox. Small, expert,
  directly on-topic.
- **TI-Planet's Discord** — same idea, larger, francophone-leaning.
- **Subject-help servers** (maths, physics, chemistry homework help) — treat exactly like the
  Reddit comment strategy: answer questions, don't advertise.
- **Large study servers** (e.g. Study Together and similar) — big but diffuse; usually have a
  promo channel where a post will be seen by approximately nobody.
- **AP-subject servers** — high volume, wrong demographic for paid conversion, and the highest
  sensitivity to anything resembling exam misconduct.

**Effort:** low per server, but Discord rewards presence over posting, which is expensive in
time. **Impact:** low. **Risk:** low. **Verdict:** do the two calculator servers, skip the
rest unless one of them happens to be somewhere you already spend time.

## 6. SEO and content

You already own a domain-worthy asset: a public GitHub repo plus a landing page. Build on it
rather than starting a blog from scratch.

**The highest-value pages to write**, in priority order:

1. **"How to put Python programs on a TI-84 Plus CE"** — the same topic as the YouTube video.
   Text and video reinforce each other and target the same query.
2. **"What can the TI-84 Plus CE Python Edition actually do?"** — captures the enormous
   population of people who own the device and have never opened the Python app. This is the
   awareness-stage query, and there's very little good writing on it.
3. **Per-program pages**, one each for the handful with real search demand: *TI-84 Newton's
   method program*, *TI-84 Simpson's rule program*, *TI-84 matrix inverse program*,
   *TI-84 kinematics program*. Each should include an actual explanation of the method, a real
   screenshot of the output, and a link to the relevant bundle. These are low-volume,
   dead-simple to rank for, and every visitor is exactly the right person.
4. **"TI-84 Plus CE Python: what's available and what isn't"** — the module list, the memory
   limits, the AppVar storage model, the `eval()` pattern. You already know all of this from
   building the library, it's genuinely poorly documented elsewhere, and it earns links from
   the enthusiast communities.

**Where to publish:** add them as pages under `storefront/` (GitHub Pages serves them for
free) and link them from the landing page footer. GitHub's own domain authority gives you a
head start you would not get from a new domain.

**Also:** put a proper description and topic tags on the GitHub repo itself, and add the
landing-page URL to the repo's About sidebar. GitHub repos rank surprisingly well for niche
technical queries.

**Effort:** ~2 h per page. **Impact:** nothing for 2–3 months, then compounding.
**Risk:** none. **Verdict:** write pages 1 and 2 during launch fortnight, the rest at one a
week afterwards.

## 7. Tutors and tutoring centres

The most overlooked channel here, and structurally the best one: a tutor who likes your
toolkit recommends it to every student they see, for years. One tutor is worth a hundred
TikTok viewers.

**Who to contact:**

- Independent maths/physics tutors on Wyzant, Superprof, and similar directories, filtered to
  those who mention the TI-84.
- Community-college and university tutoring centres and maths labs — email the coordinator.
- High-school AP maths and physics teachers, especially anyone who has posted TI-84 material
  publicly.
- YouTube channels that teach TI-84 usage — they need content and you have a product that
  makes an episode.

**How to approach:** offer the complete toolkit **free**, with no strings, in exchange for
honest feedback. Not an affiliate pitch, not a "please promote this." The free copy is cheap
for you and the reciprocity is real.

**The email:**

> Subject: Free TI-84 CE Python toolkit for your students — feedback welcome
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I've built a library of 24 Python programs for the TI-84 Plus CE Python Edition — numerical
> methods, kinematics, circuits, gas laws, that sort of thing. Plain `.py` source, so students
> can read the algorithm rather than just get an answer out of it.
>
> I'd like to give you the complete set free, no conditions. If it's useful with your
> students, great; if you tell me what's wrong with it, more useful still.
>
> [LINK]
>
> One thing I'm careful about: these are positioned strictly as homework and practice tools,
> with a disclaimer telling students to check their own exam's calculator policy. I'm not
> marketing them as exam-legal anything.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

**Why the last paragraph matters:** it is the sentence that makes an educator willing to
associate with you. Educators are the most sceptical audience for a product like this and the
most valuable one if you win them.

**Effort:** ~10 personalised emails a week, 45 min. **Impact:** slow, compounding, highest
lifetime value per contact. **Risk:** none.

## 8. Etsy search is a channel, not a storefront

Worth saying explicitly because it's easy to treat Etsy as passive. Etsy's internal search is
one of only two places (with YouTube) where people type a purchase-intent query and see your
product. Getting the listings live, fully tagged and well-photographed **is** a traffic
strategy, and it's the one that requires no ongoing effort.

See `SEO_KEYWORDS.md` for the tag sets and `SETUP_CHECKLIST.md` for the listing mechanics. The
single highest-impact detail is the **main listing photo** — a real photo of a real
calculator running a real program.

Also check **Etsy Stats → Search terms** weekly once you're live. It tells you the actual
queries that brought people to your listings, which is free, real keyword data specific to
your products. Feed it back into your tags.

## 10. Two-week launch sequence

Assumes the products and the landing page are ready to go on day 1. Roughly 1–2 hours a day.

### Week 1 — infrastructure and the assets that compound

**Day 1 — Store setup.** Work through `SETUP_CHECKLIST.md` §1–2. Gumroad account, payout
details submitted (this can take days to clear — do it first), all six products created, the
free product live. Buy your own free product to test the flow.

**Day 2 — Etsy.** Shop created, policies written, all five paid listings live with 13 tags
each from `SEO_KEYWORDS.md`. Opt out of Offsite Ads.

**Day 3 — Photos.** Shoot the real calculator running real programs. Five images per listing.
This is a real day of work and it is the difference between a listing that converts and one
that doesn't. Retro-fit the images to both platforms.

**Day 4 — Landing page live.** Paste the real product URLs into `index.html`, wire the free
form to Gumroad, deploy via `DEPLOY.md`. Update the GitHub repo description and About sidebar
to link it.

**Day 5 — Email automation.** Build the Gumroad Workflows from `DEMO_SCRIPTS.md` Part 3.
Send yourself through the whole sequence and read the emails on a phone.

**Day 6 — The YouTube tutorial.** Shoot, edit and publish the install video. Full
description, free-pack link, pinned comment, timestamps. Publish Script 5 as a Short at the
same time.

**Day 7 — Rules reconnaissance.** Work through §2's verification procedure for every
subreddit on your list and write down what you find. Read Cemetech's posting rules. Read
ticalc.org's submission criteria. **Post nothing today.** This is the day that prevents the
mistakes.

### Week 2 — distribution

**Day 8 — ticalc.org.** Submit two or three substantial programs free, with the repo linked.

**Day 9 — Cemetech.** Post Draft C in *Your Projects*. Then stay in the thread all day and
answer everything. Don't post anywhere else today.

**Day 10 — Short-form.** Publish Scripts 1 and 2. Reply to every comment.

**Day 11 — Reddit, carefully.** Only in whichever community your Day 7 research showed
actually permits it, and only Draft A, and only in one place. Simultaneously begin the comment
strategy: find three genuine TI-84 questions and answer them properly.

**Day 12 — Tutors.** Ten personalised emails. Free complete toolkit, no conditions.

**Day 13 — Short-form.** Scripts 3 and 4. Start the first SEO article ("How to put Python
programs on a TI-84 Plus CE").

**Day 14 — Review.** Pull the numbers and answer one question honestly: *which channel showed
a pulse?* Then commit the following month to that channel and stop spreading yourself thin.

### What to measure

Track weekly, in a spreadsheet, five numbers:

1. Free-pack downloads
2. Paid sales, by product
3. Landing-page visitors, by source
4. Etsy listing views and favourites (Etsy Stats)
5. Email list size and reply count

**Kill criteria, decided in advance so you don't rationalise later:** if a channel has had 4
weeks of genuine effort and produced neither sales nor free-pack signups, stop doing it. If
the free pack is getting downloads but nothing converts to paid, the problem is the product
page or the price, not the traffic — fix that before adding channels. If nothing at all moves
in 60 days, the honest conclusion is that the demand is thinner than the competitor's pricing
suggests, and the right response is to make the free tier bigger and rebuild around the
YouTube channel rather than to push harder on the same funnel.

## 11. Things not to do

- **Don't buy reviews, fabricate testimonials, or invent download counts.** Beyond the ethics,
  Etsy suspends shops for review manipulation, and a single fake claim discovered in this
  niche — where the buyers are smart and the enthusiast community is small — costs more
  credibility than every honest post you'll make.
- **Don't market on exam legality.** See `MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md` in the repo root for the
  sourced version of why. The short version: "FE exam" and "AP-exam-legal" are the two claims
  most likely to cause a buyer a real, provable loss, and both are false.
- **Don't spam multiple subreddits on the same day.** It is the single most reliable way to
  get a domain blacklisted site-wide.
- **Don't argue about cheating in public threads.** State the study-aid framing once, link the
  policy, move on.
- **Don't discount below $9.** Fixed platform fees make cheap digital products worse than
  worthless, and a low price signals low quality in a category where buyers can't inspect the
  goods before buying.
- **Don't build a second product until one channel works.** The library is finished. The
  bottleneck is distribution, and no amount of new code fixes a distribution problem.
