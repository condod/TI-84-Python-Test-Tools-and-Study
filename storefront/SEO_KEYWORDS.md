# Marketplace SEO & Keyword Research

Keyword sets for Etsy tags, Gumroad tags, listing titles, and the landing page's title and
meta description.

**Sourcing:** every claim is marked **VERIFIED** (I retrieved the page and quote it),
**SECONDARY** (a third party reports it consistently), or **INFERRED** (my reasoning). I do
not have access to a keyword tool, so **there are no search-volume numbers anywhere in this
document.** Priorities are argued from evidence, not from fabricated metrics. Anyone who
hands you a volume figure for "ti 84 calculus program" without a tool is making it up.

**What this tells you, and it is a lot:**

- **The formula is `[Specific topic] Program for TI-84 Plus CE Calculator`.** Topic first,
  device second. Not clever, not branded — a description of the thing being searched for.
- **Both `TI84` and `TI-84` spellings appear**, sometimes within the same shop. Buyers type
  both. Cover both across your title and tags.
- **Titles are short**, nowhere near Etsy's 140-character limit. These sellers are not
  keyword-stuffing, which suggests either that the niche is uncompetitive enough not to need
  it, or that they're leaving room on the table. **INFERRED:** given how thin this niche is,
  a fuller, still-readable title is likely an advantage.
- **They sell single-topic programs, and a "Combo Pack" is their bundle language.** Your
  6-program subject bundles are a much bigger unit than anything listed here.
- **VERIFIED, and important:** these listings market themselves on the ACT and SAT
  ("great for ... standardized tests like the ACT and the SAT", "Great for precalculus and
  trigonometry courses and the ACT!"). **Do not copy this.** See §6 — those are the exact
  claims the repo's `MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md` identifies as both inaccurate and a
  trademark problem.

### 1.2 Etsy's own related-search terms

**VERIFIED** — Etsy renders internal "related searches" at the bottom of listing pages
(`ref=lp_queries_internal_bottom`). On the TI-84 listings above, these surfaced:

- `ti 84 plus calculator`
- `ti-84 plus calculator`
- `ti-84 plus ce calculator`

and the corresponding Etsy market page `etsy.com/market/ti_84_plus_ce_calculator`.

This is Etsy telling you, from its own query logs, which strings its buyers use. Note what's
in them: **the device name, spelled out, with and without the hyphen.** No subject words at
all. **INFERRED implication:** on Etsy, device-name terms are the trunk of the tree and
subject terms are the branches — so every listing should carry device-name tags *and*
subject tags, and never spend all 13 slots on one or the other.

### 1.3 The vocabulary gap: buyers say "apps," sellers say "programs"

**VERIFIED**, and this is the single most useful finding here. The direct competitor
published an entire article about it
(<https://mcstutoring.com/pages/ti-84-calculator-apps-vs-programs-the-ultimate-guide>),
stating:

> "The Confusion: Students search for 'Apps' (smartphone language), but Texas Instruments
> calls them 'Programs' (technical language)."

and listing the searches they see:

> "Chemistry calculator apps for stoichiometry and gas laws." / "Physics apps for TI-84 Plus
> CE for kinematics and work/energy."

A competitor who sells in this niche full-time wrote a page specifically to capture people
using the *wrong* word. That is strong evidence the "apps" phrasing carries real volume.

**Action:** use `ti 84 apps` / `calculator apps` phrasing in **tags and body copy**, even
though it's technically imprecise, and use "programs" in headings where accuracy matters.
Meet the search where it is.

### 1.5 Enthusiast-archive vocabulary

**VERIFIED** from ticalc.org's TI-84 Plus CE science archive
(<https://ticalc.org/pub/84pluscse/basic/science/>) and Cemetech's archive browser: files are
named and described with terms like `AP Chemistry Program`, `PhysicsCircuitSolver`,
`Chemistry Math Helper`, `Science Formulas`, and categorised as
`TI-84 Plus CE BASIC Math Programs`.

**INFERRED:** the enthusiast world's vocabulary — `8xp`, `TI-BASIC`, `flash app`, `archive`,
`ez80` — is a *different language* from the student world's. Those terms belong in your
Cemetech/ticalc posts and in the GitHub repo, **not** in Etsy tags, where they'd waste slots
on people who write their own programs rather than buy them.

Also note: the ticalc science archive is dominated by TI-BASIC programs from 2014–2021. **The
CE Python niche is materially less crowded than the TI-BASIC one** — that's your gap, and
"Python" belongs in your titles as a differentiator, not just as a compatibility note.

### 1.6 Etsy field mechanics

**VERIFIED**, consistent across multiple sources citing Etsy's own Help Center and Seller
Handbook:

| Field | Limit |
|---|---|
| Tags per listing | **13** |
| Characters per tag | **20, spaces included** |
| Multi-word tags | Allowed and recommended |
| Listing title | **140 characters** |
| Photos | ≥2000px shortest side, up to 20 |
| Video | 5–15 s, silent, one per listing |

Etsy's stated ranking factors are relevancy, listing engagement, recency, listing and shop
quality, and customer service. Guidance is unanimous on two points: **fill all 13 slots**
(each empty one is a search you can't appear in), and **vary the root words** rather than
repeating one stem across slots.

Three further points, now **VERIFIED against Etsy's own Help Center and Seller Handbook**
rather than third parties:

- **Don't repeat tags.** Etsy: *"The 13 tags you add should all be as unique as possible."*
  Also don't duplicate your categories and attributes, which already function as tags.
- **Keyword position in the title does not affect ranking.** Etsy states this outright:
  *"Where a phrase is used in your title doesn't affect a listing's ranking."* Nearly every
  third-party 2026 SEO blog insists you must front-load your primary keyword "for ranking."
  **Etsy's own documentation contradicts them.** Front-load anyway — but for the *human*,
  because mobile results truncate around 35–50 characters. Don't buy a "title rewrite for
  ranking" service sold on the other premise.
- **Etsy auto-badges digital downloads in search results.** Putting "Digital Download" or
  "Instant Download" in the *title* burns roughly 18 characters on something the interface
  already shows. Keep it in tags.
- Etsy's August 2025 title guidance also says to remove subjective words ("perfect," "best,"
  "ultimate") from titles and avoid repeating words within one.

wanted a quadratic solver badly enough to type one in by hand. That is the single strongest
argument for leading the free starter pack with the quadratic solver. By contrast the
subject-specific program videos sit in the hundreds to low thousands, and your competitor's
own install tutorial has **1.4K views after five months** — the attention in this niche is
concentrated in *installation and novelty*, and it is essentially unclaimed.

### 1.8 Marketplace density: how empty the shelves actually are

**VERIFIED by direct search.**

- **Gumroad Discover is empty for this niche.** "ti 84 calculator" returns **3 products, none
  TI-84 related**; "graphing calculator" returns 9, none relevant. You would be the only
  TI-84 program seller there.
- **But Discover is gated.** Eligibility requires completed payout settings, **≥$100 in
  genuine sales** (self-purchases don't count), and passing a risk review that averages around
  three weeks. Products additionally need at least one non-refunded sale, a selected category,
  and **ratings enabled** — turning ratings off removes you from Discover search entirely.
  **So at launch, Gumroad is a Google-SEO asset and a checkout, not a discovery channel.**
  Write the product title and first paragraph for Google, not for Discover. Discover becomes
  relevant later, and when it does, the shelf is bare.
- Gumroad allows **5 tags** per product, set under Product → Share → Gumroad Discover, and
  each tag has its own `gumroad.com/discover/[tag]` page. Keyword-stuffing is actively
  penalised, so with five slots precision beats coverage.
- **Teachers Pay Teachers is where this category actually has depth, and the prices are
  brutal:** single-topic TI-84 programs list at **$1–$2** ("Quadratic Formula Program for
  TI-84 CE," $1.00; "Solve triangles - Law of Sines / Cosines," $2.00). There is also a
  40-program TI-83/84 bundle from a seller called Infinity. TPT isn't a channel I'd recommend
  — but if a buyer price-anchors on TPT rather than on mcstutoring's $50–$160, your $12–$19
  looks expensive rather than cheap. That's an argument for making the bundle's size and the
  drag-and-drop install obvious in the first line of every listing: 52 programs at $49 is
  under a dollar each, which compares well even against TPT's $1–$2 singles.

Lower intent, far less competition, and each one is cheap to target with a tag or a content

`stoichiometry calculator program` · `ti 84 formula program`

**`how to put programs on ti 84` deserves special attention.** It's informational rather than

### Tier 3 — enthusiast terms (use only outside the marketplaces)

`8xv` · `8xp` · `TI-BASIC` · `ez80` · `ti_plotlib` · `CE Python AppVar` ·
`TI-84 Plus CE homebrew` · `calculator programming`

For GitHub topics, Cemetech and ticalc posts. **Never in Etsy tags** — these people are

**One exception worth making:** `.8xv` is worth mentioning in your Etsy and Gumroad
*descriptions* (not tags — the term is far too rare to justify one of 13 slots). The bundles
ship a ready-to-install `.8xv` Python AppVar for every program alongside the `.py` source,
and "drag the file onto your calculator" is a concretely better install story than the
copy-paste-into-the-Program-Editor process that every competitor listing describes. That is a
conversion argument rather than a keyword, so put it in the first two lines of the
description and on the contents image, where buyers who are weighing up listings will see it.

### Terms to avoid entirely

See §6. Short version: no exam brands, anywhere.

### 3.10 Notes on these sets

- **`ti 84 plus ce python` is exactly 20 characters** — it fits, with zero to spare. Do not
  add a word to it.
- **`ti 84 calculator` repeats across sets deliberately.** Etsy's advice against repeating
  roots applies *within* a listing, not across listings. The device term is your trunk; every
  listing needs it.
- **The hyphen question.** Etsy tags support hyphens within a word but not leading. Etsy's
  matching is fairly tolerant of the `ti 84` / `ti-84` / `ti84` variation, so spending three
  separate slots on spelling variants would be wasteful. Cover the variants in the **title**,
  where they cost nothing.
- **`ti connect ce` is a CE-only signal, and that is the point.** It appears in the Complete
  Toolkit and Free Starter Pack sets, and `ti connect ce python` sits in tier 2. None of them
  reach an Evo owner, who uses TI's web app instead — which is correct, because the `.8xv`
  AppVars that make the install a drag-and-drop only work on CE hardware. Keep the tag.
- **No `evo`, `ti 84 evo` or `ti connect evo` in any tag set yet.** Recommendation, reasoning
  and the condition for revisiting it are in §6.1.
- **Fill the Attributes and Materials fields too.** They're indexed and they're free.

## 6. Terms to avoid — and why

The repo's [`MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md`](../MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md) documents this with
primary sources. The keyword-specific consequences:

| Do not use | Reason |
|---|---|
| `ap calculus`, `ap physics`, `ap chemistry`, `ap exam` in **tags, keywords, meta tags, shop name or domain** | College Board's trademark guidelines specifically prohibit use of its marks "as part of company names, company taglines or slogans, social media pages, Internet domain names, web site addresses, or meta tags." |
| `sat`, `psat`, `sat math` | Same trademark problem, plus College Board's SAT rules require removing programs with algebra functionality and clearing saved formulas. The claim would be false as well as infringing. |
| `act`, `act math` | ACT's Terms of Use state that "No use of any ACT trademark is permitted without express written consent." Additionally ACT caps permitted programs at 25 logical lines — your programs don't qualify. |
| `fe exam`, `pe exam`, `ncees`, `engineering licensure` | The TI-84 is not an approved calculator for NCEES exams at all. This is the claim most likely to cause a buyer a real, provable loss. |
| `exam legal`, `exam approved`, `test approved`, `proctor safe` | No exam board approves third-party calculator programs. The claim is simply false. |
| `cheat`, `cheat sheet`, `hack`, `test hack` | Invites removal on both marketplaces, and ticalc.org explicitly rejects files "intended primarily to facilitate cheating on school assignments or tests." |
| `evo`, `ti 84 evo`, `ti connect evo` in **tags, titles or meta description** | Not a compliance landmine like the rows above, but wrong for now on three counts — claim accuracy, trademark placement and slot economics. Full reasoning immediately below; this one is explicitly revisitable. |

### 6.1 "evo" — keep it out of tags, put it into content

**Recommendation (decided 2026-08-12): no `evo` in any paid-listing tag, title or meta
description; yes to a dedicated Evo content page.** The evidence behind the compatibility
position is in [`../business/EVO_TRANSITION.md`](../business/EVO_TRANSITION.md).

Three reasons to keep it out of the marketplace fields:

1. **A tag is read as a compatibility claim, and this one isn't tested.** Etsy and Gumroad
   shoppers treat tags as "this is for my device." Evo compatibility here is *expected* — the
   programs import only `math`, `random` and `time`, the two TI-specific imports are guarded
   with fallbacks, nothing depends on screen size, and TI Connect Evo converts `.py` on send —
   but **nobody has run these on Evo hardware**, and the `.8xv` AppVars that carry half the
   value proposition definitively do **not** work on an Evo, which uses `.8xv2`. Tagging for
   Evo traffic buys refunds and one-star reviews from buyers who were told the product was for
   them.
2. **Trademark placement.** TI-84 Evo™ and TI Connect™ Evo are Texas Instruments marks.
   [`MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md`](../MARKETING_CLAIMS_GUIDE.md) §6.2 applies the strictest
   published policy in the set uniformly — no third-party marks in tags, meta tags, shop names
   or domains — and tags and meta descriptions are exactly the placements it names. Naming the
   Evo *factually in body copy*, next to the non-affiliation footer, is fine and is already
   done on the landing page and in every bundle README.
3. **Slot economics.** Thirteen tags is a hard cap, and Evo owners searching for *programs*
   barely exist yet: Cemetech's Evo Python archive held one hobby game months after launch. A
   speculative tag would displace a proven CE term for traffic that isn't there.

**Where `evo` does belong is organic content**, because a page can be accurate about
uncertainty in a way a 20-character tag cannot. "**Do these programs work on the TI-84 Evo?**"
is a question students will type in rising volume as Evo units reach classrooms, almost nobody
has published an answer, and the honest answer is genuinely useful: the `.py` files are
expected to work and transfer through `connectevo.ti.com`, the `.8xv` files do not, here is
how to check on your own calculator, and TI-BASIC programs from other sellers need conversion
before they will run at all. The page title is in §7 and the article is item 5 in
[`LAUNCH_PLAN.md`](LAUNCH_PLAN.md) §6.

**Revisit this the moment there is an Evo hardware test pass.** At that point `ti 84 evo`
becomes both defensible and unusually uncontested — which is a reason to run the test, not a
reason to pre-empt it.

## 8. What to do once you have real data

Everything above is inference from competitor behaviour and verified platform mechanics.
Within a few weeks you will have something better: **your own query data.**

- **Etsy Stats → Search terms** lists the actual queries that brought visitors to each
  listing. This is real, free, product-specific keyword data. Check it weekly.
- **Gumroad** reports views and conversion per product, which tells you which *positioning*
  works even when it can't tell you which query drove it.
- **YouTube Studio → Traffic source → YouTube search** shows the exact phrases people searched
  before landing on your install video. For the "how do I put programs on a TI-84" family of
  queries, this will be the highest-quality keyword data you ever get.

**Revisit this document once a month for the first quarter.** Swap any tag that Etsy Stats
shows has never produced an impression for a phrase that has. Tag optimisation is an
iterative loop with real feedback, not a one-time guess — treat the sets in §3 as a starting
hypothesis, not an answer.
